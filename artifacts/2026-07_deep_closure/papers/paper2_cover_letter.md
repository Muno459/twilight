# Cover letter, Paper 2 (application)

To the Editors, Journal of Quantitative Spectroscopy and Radiative Transfer

Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "When does dawn become visible?
The Quranic dawn (fajr al-sadiq) and dusk (shafaq) as a detection problem,
tested against 116 years of observation," for publication in JQSRT.

Prayer timekeeping for roughly two billion people rests on twilight
events that the defining sources describe as visual detections, yet
modern practice replaces them with fixed solar depression angles that
disagree by tens of minutes and become undefined toward the poles. The
manuscript formulates the dawn criterion as a quantitative psychophysical
detection problem on absolute simulated sky radiance (supplied by the
companion methods manuscript), with a single calibrated constant bridging
laboratory contrast thresholds to field detection. Calibrated once on the
campaigns that document a central-mean dawn depression, the model
reproduces sixteen published campaigns spanning 116 years and 58 degrees
of latitude at a median absolute residual of 0.26 degrees of depression
over the fourteen scored rows,
and reproduces a year-long out-of-sample panel study at Birmingham,
including its double-peaked seasonal curve, with zero retuning. On the
same benchmark the fixed angles in worldwide use miss by 1.6 to 4.4
degrees RMS where they exist; the most widely used one is undefined on
15 of the 42 panel dates, and even the 15-degree convention on 8.

The companion methods manuscript, submitted alongside, carries the
transport model and its validation ladder; the two are written to stand
alone but review naturally as a pair, and we would be grateful if the
editors could assign them together.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all
results are reproducible from the public code repository referenced in the
text. The author declares no competing interests. Computed times are
offered as a research instrument for observers, calendar committees, and
jurists, not as a replacement for established calendars; the manuscript
states this explicitly.

Sincerely,
Mostafa Mahdi
University of Copenhagen
